              What do Gi* and Local Moran's I actually mean?

              Hotspots are not just "low scores." Both statistics compare each cell to its neighbours, computed on real hex geometry (even though the input latency values they're computed over are the simulated panel — see the Data Quality tab).

              Getis-Ord Gi* (the "hotspot" color-by mode): if a cell is bad AND its neighbours are also bad, it's a statistically significant cluster — hot_99/hot_95/hot_90 by confidence level. A bad cell surrounded by fine neighbours is not_significant — probably a one-off reading, not a real problem area.

              Local Moran's I (the "moran" color-by mode) adds a second axis — whether the cell agrees or disagrees with its neighbours:

              • HH — bad cell, bad neighbours (a genuine cluster, same idea as Gi* hot)
              • LL — good cell, good neighbours (a healthy cluster)
              • HL — bad cell, good neighbours (a surgical, single-cell fix — the neighbourhood is fine, so a targeted intervention is more likely to work than a broad one)
              • LH — good cell, bad neighbours (worth watching — often becomes HH next)

              Both are computed with permutation-based inference (z-scores + p-values) on rtt_median, per spatial_processor.py.
